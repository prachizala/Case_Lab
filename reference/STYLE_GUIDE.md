# SBS CaseLab - CE Case Pack Pedagogical Style Guide

This style guide defines the institutional standard for all Continuous Evaluation (CE) Case Packs generated within SBS CaseLab. Every generated pack must follow this single cohesive pedagogical standard across Finance, HR, and Marketing.

---

## 1. Case Philosophy & Overview

A CE Case Pack is a rigorous, curriculum-aligned academic assessment instrument consisting of three interconnected components:
1. **The Teaching Case**: An authentic, past-tense business narrative highlighting multi-faceted strategic and operational dilemmas.
2. **Examination Questions**: Two demanding, Bloom-level assessment questions testing both critical analysis and managerial evaluation.
3. **Faculty Model Answer Key & Teaching Notes**: A comprehensive, evidence-grounded benchmark answer key with rubric-aligned criteria and marking schemes.

---

## 2. Case Architecture & Structural Sections

Every teaching case must strictly adhere to the following section hierarchy:

### I. Title
- Clear, professional title featuring the focal organization, core dilemma, and strategic domain.
- *Example*: `Financing Growth at Shakti Engineering Ltd.`

### II. Background of the Company
- Founding year, geographical headquarters (Indian state/city), core product or service offerings.
- Protagonist identity: Named founder, Managing Director, or executive.
- Growth trajectory and scale: Market expansion, customer segments, distribution networks, export initiatives.
- Transition point: How rapid commercial success created financial, operational, or organizational friction that legacy processes cannot sustain.

### III. Core Problem / Decision Sections (2 to 4 Titled Sections)
Each distinct strategic decision or dilemma must have its own titled section containing four essential elements:
1. **The Situation**: Concrete operational bottleneck or capital need (with specific metrics: working capital tie-up, equipment cost, headcount attrition, market share pressure).
2. **Options Considered**: Minimum of 2–3 managerial alternatives evaluated by the leadership team (e.g., bank loan vs. lease vs. hire purchase; internal promotion vs. lateral hire; direct distribution vs. distributor network).
3. **Choice & Mechanism**: The chosen instrument or strategy, explaining the operational mechanics (how invoices are factored, how equipment is leased, how appraisals are structured).
4. **Operational Outcome**: How the immediate bottleneck was resolved, liquidity injected, talent stabilized, or market share defended.

### IV. Strategic Decisions Summary & Risk Synthesis
- A consolidated recap table or summary detailing how each decision contributed to institutional growth.
- **Costs, Risks & Limitations**: A dedicated examination of trade-offs (e.g., factoring fees, non-recourse forfaiting discounts, long-term leasing cost premiums, cultural friction in 360 appraisals, channel conflict in omnichannel expansion).
- Concluding dilemma emphasizing that management must continuously balance risk, return, and long-term sustainability.

### V. Mandatory Data Tables
- Every case must include at least one structured data table:
  - **Finance**: Financial extracts (receivables aging, cash flows, loan terms, lease vs. buy cash outflows).
  - **HR**: Workforce metrics (headcount, attrition rates, cost-per-hire, eNPS scores).
  - **Marketing**: Market data (market share %, competitor price points, channel mix, segment demographics).

---

## 3. Length & Narrative Tone

- **Standard Case**: 1,000 to 1,300 words (excluding exam questions and answer keys).
- **Short Case (FlashCase)**: Approximately 550 to 650 words with 1 focused decision dilemma.
- **Tense**: Strict past tense throughout the narrative (`"Shakti Engineering entered into..."`, `"Management evaluated..."`).
- **Tone**: Objective, academic, third-person narrative. Plain language appropriate for postgraduate management students (MBA/PGDM/BCA). Avoid journalistic hyperbole, flowery buzzwords, or first-person editorializing.
- **Context**: Rooted in Indian enterprise reality (INR Crore/Lakh denominations, Tier-1/Tier-2 geographic hubs, Indian regulatory environments like RBI, SEBI, MCA, DPDP Act).

---

## 4. Examination Questions Style

Standard packs contain **exactly 2 exam questions** (FlashCases contain 1 question):
- **Bloom's Taxonomy Verbs**: Every question must begin with or prominently feature an explicit high-order cognitive verb (*Analyze*, *Compare*, *Evaluate*, *Justify*, *Calculate*, *Recommend*, *Diagnose*).
- **Explicit Concepts Named**: The question must explicitly cite the curricular concepts and instruments from the case (e.g., *"Analyze how factoring and forfaiting helped..."* instead of *"Discuss how financing helped..."*).
- **Question 1 (Analytical / Comparative)**: Focuses on comparing mechanisms, evaluating structural differences, or calculating quantitative trade-offs.
- **Question 2 (Evaluative / Justification)**: Focuses on critical judgment (`"In your opinion, were these appropriate strategies for the company's growth? Justify your answer with reasons."`).
- **Marking Allocation**: Default 20 marks per question (Total 40 marks) for standard Continuous Evaluation (CE-3 / CE-4) sets.

---

## 5. Model Answer Key Style (Faculty Only)

Model answers must serve as authoritative grading benchmarks (350 to 600 words per question) structured as follows:

1. **Introduction**: 1 concise paragraph framing the protagonist's strategic situation and defining the core concepts being assessed.
2. **Numbered / Thematic Sections**:
   - Distinct sections corresponding to each evaluated instrument or topic.
   - For evaluation questions, each option must include:
     - `Evaluation`: 3–4 bullet points analyzing operational benefits and strategic alignment.
     - `Limitation`: Dedicated explicit identification of financial, legal, or cultural costs.
     - `Overall Verdict`: Clear evaluative conclusion (e.g., *"Overall: A suitable short-term liquidity intervention."*).
3. **Structured Comparison Table (for Compare questions)**:
   - Must contain columns: `Basis of Comparison | Option A | Option B`.
   - Minimum 4–5 analytical dimensions (e.g., Purpose, Risk Bearing, Credit Period, Operational Services, Balance Sheet Impact).
4. **Quantitative Mathematical Working (for Quantitative topics)**:
   - Must provide explicit formula, inputs extracted from case, step-by-step arithmetic working, calculated output with units, and managerial decision rule.
5. **Overall Assessment**: Synthesis of how the individual choices integrate into holistic corporate strategy.
6. **Conclusion**: Pragmatic executive takeaway emphasizing risk controls and governance.

---

## 6. Confidentiality & Separation of Concerns

- **Faculty / Admin Access Only**: The Model Answer Key, Teaching Notes, Rubrics, and Validation Checks must NEVER be served to student sessions, student exports, or unauthenticated public endpoints.
- **Student Cohort Visibility**: Students enrolled in courses receive only the approved Teaching Case, Data Tables, and Examination Questions.
